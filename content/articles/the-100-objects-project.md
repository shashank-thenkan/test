# The 100 Objects Project

What makes an ordinary object worth remembering?

A ticket from a train journey. A key that no longer opens anything. A receipt from a restaurant you visited years ago.

Most objects eventually disappear from our lives without much ceremony. But some of them carry memories, stories and details that are difficult to reconstruct once they're gone.

The 100 Objects Project is a simple creative experiment: document one hundred ordinary objects and investigate the stories behind them.

## The idea

Each object becomes a small entry in a growing visual archive.

The entry includes:

- A photograph of the object.
- Its name and approximate date.
- Where it came from.
- The story or memory associated with it.
- One unexpected detail discovered during documentation.

The objective isn't to collect expensive or particularly beautiful things. Quite the opposite.

The more ordinary the object, the more interesting the exercise becomes.

## The process

The project follows three simple rules.

**1. Document, don't curate.**

Choose objects from everyday life rather than selecting only things that look good in photographs.

**2. Find the story.**

Ask where the object came from, who made it, why it exists and what makes it worth keeping.

**3. Keep the archive consistent.**

Every object gets the same basic documentation format, allowing the collection to develop into a coherent body of work.

## What could come out of it?

One hundred entries could eventually become a photographic essay, a small printed catalogue, an interactive archive or even an exhibition.

But those outcomes aren't the starting point.

The first objective is simply to pay closer attention to the things that surround us.
