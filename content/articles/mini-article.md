# The Sound of Time

A mechanical clock doesn't simply measure time. It makes time audible.

The familiar ticking sound comes from the escapement, a mechanism that regulates the release of energy from the clock's mainspring or weight. It allows the gears to advance in controlled intervals rather than spinning freely.

But here's the interesting part: the ticking isn't an essential feature of timekeeping. It's a by-product of the mechanism.

Quartz clocks work differently. Their timekeeping relies on the regular vibration of a quartz crystal, which is converted into electrical pulses. Many digital clocks therefore make no sound at all.

So the next time you hear a clock ticking in an otherwise quiet room, remember that you're not really hearing time pass.

You're hearing a machine negotiate with it.
